Steven Spielberg

American filmmaker • Born December 18, 1946, Cincinnati, Ohio

At a Glance

Spielberg is a leading figure of New Hollywood and a pioneer of the modern blockbuster. He is widely seen as one of cinema’s most influential directors and is the highest-grossing film director of all time, with worldwide earnings near $11 billion.

Net worth: at least $5.3 billion (Forbes)

Rare honor: one of 22 people to achieve EGOT status

Major Honors

  • 3 Academy Awards
  • 4 Golden Globes, 4 BAFTAs, 12 Emmys
  • Tony Award and Grammy Award
  • AFI Life Achievement Award (1995)
  • Honorary knighthood (2001)
  • Kennedy Center Honor (2006)
  • Cecil B. DeMille Award (2009)
  • Presidential Medal of Freedom (2015)
  • National Medal of Arts (2023)
  • Time 100 Impact Award (2023)

Early Life and Start

He grew up in Haddonfield, New Jersey, and Phoenix, Arizona, then studied film in California. His career began in television. His first notable directing work was the TV film Duel (1971). His first theatrical feature was The Sugarland Express (1974), which started his long partnership with composer John Williams.

Breakthrough Films

Jaws (1975) made him a household name. He followed it with major hits: Close Encounters of the Third Kind (1977), E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial (1982), and the original Indiana Jones trilogy (1981–1989).

In the 1980s he also turned to drama with The Color Purple (1985) and Empire of the Sun (1987).

Later Work

Schindler’s List (1993) and Saving Private Ryan (1998) won him Best Director Oscars. Schindler’s List also won Best Picture and is often named among the greatest films ever made.

Science fiction: Jurassic Park (1993), A.I. (2001), Minority Report (2002), War of the Worlds (2005), Ready Player One (2018), Disclosure Day (2026).

Historical drama: Amistad, Munich, War Horse, Lincoln, Bridge of Spies, The Post.

Other notable films: Catch Me If You Can, The Terminal, The Adventures of Tintin, West Side Story (2021), The Fabelmans (2022).

Producer and Companies

He co-founded Amblin Entertainment and DreamWorks Pictures. As a producer he helped launch hits such as Poltergeist, Gremlins, Back to the Future, Who Framed Roger Rabbit, Twister, Transformers, plus TV series like Animaniacs and Band of Brothers.

Legacy

Several of his films rank among the greatest and highest-grossing ever. Seven have been added to the U.S. National Film Registry. In 2013, Time named him one of the 100 most influential people.
